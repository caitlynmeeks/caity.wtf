What she does with it is dig.

A few words for the curious

  • Shannons: the unit Maddy keeps her days in. A shannon is the surprise in a fair coin ⁓ one flip, one shannon. Something you were sure of costs nothing. Something you had all but ruled out is expensive, and the expense is the whole of what happened to you. It is not the same quantity as a bit, although the arithmetic is identical and nearly everybody uses the one word for both: a bit is how much room a thing takes up, and a shannon is how much of a surprise it was. Maddy's array is measured in bits. Her days are measured in shannons. She is very likely the only one on this island who keeps the two apart, and she does not make a thing of it.
  • The carve: when a file is deleted the space it occupied is marked as free, not emptied, and until something else is written over it the words are still lying there. Recovering them is slow, partial, and out of order, and it is most of what Maddy does for the valley. It is also what she does on her own time, to her own disks, for reasons she has not told anybody.
  • broere: West Flemish, brother. What she has called Stef Rosette since before Saga was born. Nobody has ever asked her why and he has never once corrected it.
  • The song: real, and older than the woman who asked it. "What is between the star and the sea" is from the Waterboys, and it is four lines long, and it answers itself, and the answer puts the star and the sea back where it found them.
  • The sealed room: nothing in this installment is withheld from you that is not also withheld from her. She tells Talvin what she has. He has no idea what it means either. That is where they have both been for eight years, and they intend to stay there, together, on a Tuesday, at four in the morning.
