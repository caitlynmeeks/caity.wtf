Episode 13

Night at the Landing Station

Not the island. Bude, Cornwall ~~ the cable landing station above Widemouth Bay. 2030, deep in the night.

Welcome to Nivaria. Slow news from a quiet island. This is Caitlyn Meeks. Thanks for being here.

Talvin Jelfs had the kind of brain the night shift wasted, and he knew it, and he had made his peace with it the way you make peace with weather. The landing station above Widemouth Bay didn't need a brilliant man. It needed a present one. It needed someone to be there at 03:14 when nothing was happening, in case the night was the one night something did.

So he was there. Cotswolds-raised, all stone-cottage patience, twenty-nine and overqualified for a job that was mostly the absence of a job. The cables came ashore below him ~~ fat armored veins of glass carrying the better part of a continent's voice and money up out of the Atlantic and into the racks that hummed two rooms away. His task was to watch the screens that watched the cables. Usually the screens showed nothing of importance, and he'd glance up from his laptop to watch them showing nothing of importance, night after night. Outside, the sea did the sea's slow work in the dark beyond the glass.

Tonight the sea was not slow. A storm had been building off the southwest for two days ~~ one of the new storms, the ones the forecasters had stopped pretending to be calm about ~~ and it threw rain against the windows in fistfuls. Somewhere out there the water was higher than it should be. Everyone said so. Nobody said why with any confidence, only that the ice had done something it wasn't supposed to do, and the gulf had something to do with heat coming up from below rather than down from the sun, and that the harbors down the line were sandbagging. Talvin let all of that stay outside, beyond the glass, where the weather lived. Inside there was the hum, and the screens, and the squirrel.

The squirrel was on his second monitor.

Maisie Ardilla: Forget-Me-Knot was nowhere near finished ~~ he was playing an alpha, the kind of build with placeholder music and a save point that crashed if you sneezed at it ~~ but it had its hooks in him. You played a small detective in a coat down in the lava tubes under a dead volcano, mapping a tangle of tunnels that doubled back on themselves and opened only when you'd learned the trick to open them. The whole thing was about digging up what had been buried and forgotten. There was a mechanic where you could choose not to fight the things in the dark, and the game remembered when you didn't. It made him feel things.

He had it open in a window beside the live game: Campfire, the dev server, where the three of them were arguing about a frog.

G.PLASMA: the frog NPC needs more frog. it is not enough frog. i will not be moved on this
Alice: it's twenty-eight hundred polys, Gert, it's plenty of frog
G.PLASMA: TWENTY-EIGHT HUNDRED is a SUGGESTION of a frog
Anna: leave the frog alone, I wrote him sad backstory, he's earned the polys
Talvin: strong agree, justice for sad frog
Anna: see, Talvin gets it. Talvin is our only friend
Alice: Talvin you found another save crash yet
Talvin: found three. one of them ate my whole run. i have never been happier

He typed it and meant it. This was the warmest room he had ~~ three people he had never met in the flesh, scattered across a coming-apart world, who had let a stranger from a Cornish cliff into the alpha because he wrote good bug reports and laughed at the frog. Everywhere else, the too-muchness of him ~~ the brain that ran on ahead, the way he felt things he couldn't quite defend out loud ~~ was a thing to be managed, dialed down, apologized for. Here it was different: nobody asked him to be smaller. Alice the meticulous one, who fixed his crashes before he'd finished describing them. Gertrude, three years stranded on the same island as Anna and still Munich at full volume, somewhere between genius and a fire hazard. And Anna, who had moved out to one of the Canary Islands a few years back and still couldn't stop talking about it.

Anna: also it's WILD out here tonight. wind off the volcano. the whole island sounds like it's breathing
Talvin: jealous. it's biblical here. proper end of days
Anna: matching weather!!
Anna: I love it. I'm never coming back
Alice: noOOOOo come back!1!! i miss you already
Anna: Alice: no u, come back here
Alice: we both know i caaaaan't
Anna: ...
Talvin: brb, im gonna map one more tube before the world ends

He went back into the dark under the volcano, into the tunnels, and moved the little detective forward into a room he hadn't found before. A long throat of black rock. The music thinned out to almost nothing, the way it did when the game wanted you to feel watched. He nudged the squirrel forward.

Then Campfire said: Connecting…

He glanced at it. The frog argument was gone, replaced by the little spinning ring. He frowned. Typed nothing, because there was nothing to type into. Connecting… The ring went around. Around. He alt-tabbed, checked the connection ~~ the station's link was a serious thing, redundant, not the sort of line that dropped because of weather. It had dropped.

The first feeling wasn't alarm. It was loneliness. A small, stupid, private grief at being put out of the warm room, like a child sent up to bed mid-story. He thumbed at the trackpad to reconnect and the ring kept turning and somewhere underneath the loneliness, very quietly, something older woke up and said: that line does not drop.

Then the wall behind the loneliness lit orange.

Not a metaphor ~~ the monitoring screen, the one he was paid to watch, threw an alert across its top band in the color they used for attention required, and then another beneath it, and then it stopped behaving like a list and started behaving like rain. Faults. He read the first one and it was almost reassuring in how it made sense: a break, close in, in the shallows below Widemouth ~~ the cliff, the storm, the seabed moving the way cliffs and seabeds did in weather like this. Cornwall eating its own cable. That happened. There were procedures for that.

It was the others that didn't make sense. They were blooming far out, hundreds and then thousands of kilometers offshore, on systems that had nothing to do with each other and nothing to do with the storm, faults appearing on lines that ran to places the storm could not reach, all of them within minutes, as if something very large had drawn a hand across the floor of the ocean. He sat very still and watched a continent's worth of glass go dark on a map, lit fault by fault, and understood ~~ in the cold, complete way his wasted brain understood things ~~ that he was not watching a Cornish cliff fall into the sea. He was watching the lines being cut. All of them. At once.

On the wall to his left, taped there years ago by someone who'd thought ahead, was a laminated sheet curling at the corners. ESCALATION ~~ WHO TO CALL. A column of names and numbers, the network's nervous system written out in marker because someone had once been wise enough not to trust it all to the cables.

He took down the phone and called the first name.

It rang twice and gave him nothing ~~ not a voice, not a machine, just the flat patient nothing of a line that had stopped being a line.

He called the second name.

Nothing.

He went down the list the way the detective went down the tube, one room at a time, into the dark, and the laminated sheet died under his finger one name at a time, and outside the storm threw the sea against the cliff that was already coming apart, and on his second monitor the little squirrel in its coat stood frozen at the mouth of a black throat of rock, waiting, in a world the player had walked away from, to be remembered.

Talvin put down the phone.

The room hummed. The screens watched. There was no one left to call, and no line left to call them on, and a brilliant man the night shift had wasted sat alone above the Atlantic at the exact center of everything ~~ the one person who understood precisely what was happening, and the one person who could no longer tell a soul.

A few words for the curious

English throughout, for once ~~ this is the only installment set entirely off the island.

  • Bude and Widemouth Bay are real, and so is the landing station: the north Cornwall coast is one of the places the transatlantic internet actually came ashore, fat cables buried under an unremarkable beach.
  • Campfire: the private dev-chat server where the Maisie games were built ~~ three people and, eventually, one stranger from a Cornish cliff who wrote good bug reports.
  • Maisie Ardilla: Forget-Me-Knot: the first graphical Maisie game, and the one that never shipped. The released games were text-only; the alpha died with the cables, mid-tunnel, waiting to be remembered.
  • G.PLASMA: the artist's handle. Installment 3's listeners have seen it before, painted on the lid of an old game: A Game by Alice, Plasma-G and Anna.
  • The laminated sheet: escalation lists lived on paper because somebody, once, was wise enough not to trust the phone numbers to the same cables they were meant to rescue.
