Ten-Bel. The same night, and the morning after.

Welcome back to Nivaria. Slow news from a quiet island. This is Caitlyn Meeks. Thanks for being here.

They put La Tropa in bungalow nineteen, which had four beds, eleven people, and a smell of cupboards.

Saga lay on the floor on a folded blanket between Auxi and the wall and listened to her family not sleeping. Her father had said the sentence about being furious later and had then, being her father, actually delivered on it: they had sat on the step in the dark for half an hour and he had listened to every single thing she had noticed, and had not once told her she was imagining it, and had said at the end of it, "You are right, and it is worse than you think, and we still are not doing anything tonight, and here is why."

The why was this. Two hundred people. Sixty of them children. One vehicle. And a congregation who had, so far, committed the crime of being extremely nice.

"You cannot rescue people who do not think they are captured," Mateo said. "You will just be the man who ruined the party."

At about one in the morning Saga gave up, and got up, and went out.

The night on the dry coast is not like the night at home. There is no fog to hold the world close. It is enormous and cold and dry and the stars go all the way down to the sea, and the whole white grid of Ten-Bel lay out below her under them, silent, with the faded word on every wall.

Ikea was sitting on the wall outside bungalow nineteen.

She had been there, Saga realized, for some time.

"Your dog snores," said Ikea.

"That's Tibor."

"Ah."

In March the congregation's motorhome came up the mountain.

Everybody on this island knows that vehicle. It is a diesel Hymer from about 2009, eight meters of it, cream-colored and going chalky, with WAAKZAAM painted along both flanks in letters a meter high and a solar panel on the roof that has been there since before the Quiet. It is the outreach van. It goes out for a fortnight at a time, twice a year, up the north coast and around the Anagas, and if you have lived on Tenerife any length of time you have opened a door to it. Bolted to the back of it, above the tow bar, is a hydraulic scooter lift.
